MissionReady
An independent product discovery case study: improving aerospace engineer onboarding and knowledge access
Project type: Independent product discovery and service-design concept
Domain: Aerospace · People Operations · Digital Transformation · Product Operations
Methods: Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) · Design Thinking · Outcome-Driven Innovation (ODI)
Status: Concept proposal — research and outcomes not yet validated

Overview
MissionReady explores how a growing aerospace organization could help newly hired engineers become independently effective through a more structured onboarding experience and better access to trusted internal knowledge.

In technically complex environments, onboarding involves more than completing administrative tasks. Engineers need to understand organizational processes, locate current approved documentation, identify process owners, obtain the right access, and know where to go when they encounter blockers.

This project explores how product discovery and service-design methods can be applied to that challenge.

The proposed concept, MissionReady, combines a personalized onboarding journey, trusted knowledge discovery, guided next steps, and human escalation.

The goal is not simply to automate onboarding. It is to reduce avoidable friction while preserving engineering controls, information security, and the importance of human expertise.

The problem space
The central research question:

How might we help newly hired engineers navigate unfamiliar organizational processes and find trustworthy, relevant information without creating additional administrative work or compromising engineering controls?

The project starts with several hypotheses to investigate:

Information may be fragmented across internal systems, documents, and teams.

New engineers may struggle to identify process owners and appropriate escalation routes.

Managers and mentors may spend time answering recurring procedural questions.

Existing onboarding processes may track task completion without fully addressing operational readiness.

These are working hypotheses, not validated research findings.

Methodology
The case study combines three complementary approaches.

1. Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)
The primary job statement:

"When I join a complex engineering organization, help me become independently effective in my role by giving me timely access to trusted information, clear next steps, and the right people to contact."

JTBD is used to focus on the progress engineers need to make rather than assuming that a particular technology is the answer.

2. Universal Job Map
The engineer's journey is structured around eight functional steps:

Define

Locate

Prepare

Confirm

Execute

Monitor

Modify

Conclude

This framework helps identify potential friction throughout the job, from understanding the task to confirming successful completion.

3. Outcome-Driven Innovation (ODI)
ODI is used to prioritize potential unmet needs by considering the importance of desired outcomes and current satisfaction.

The case study includes illustrative opportunity scores to demonstrate the method. These are hypothetical examples, not survey results or validated customer insights.

Design Thinking provides the overarching discovery and iteration approach: understand users, define the problem, explore solutions, prototype, test, and learn.

Proposed solution: MissionReady
MissionReady is a conceptual onboarding and knowledge-access experience with five proposed capabilities:

Capability	Intended value
Personalized onboarding path	Role-relevant tasks, prerequisites, owners, and completion criteria
Trusted knowledge discovery	Access to authorized information with source links and document status
Guided next steps	Clearer understanding of dependencies, approvals, and required actions
Human escalation	Direct routes to the appropriate expert when information is missing or unclear
Manager visibility	A proportionate view of onboarding progress and unresolved blockers
The concept is deliberately designed around trust and governance rather than automation for its own sake.

Aerospace information security and responsible AI
Aerospace environments can involve sensitive technical information, intellectual property, export-controlled data, and safety-critical processes.

The proposed design therefore considers:

Role-based access and least-privilege permissions.

Use of approved information sources and environments.

Source traceability and document version awareness.

Clear escalation when information is ambiguous or unsupported.

No substitution of AI-generated guidance for controlled engineering procedures or formal approvals.

Proportionate and transparent manager reporting.

Any real implementation would require appropriate security, engineering, privacy, legal, and information-governance review.

Discovery and validation plan
The proposed six-week plan covers:

Phase	Focus
Week 1	Scope the problem and define research questions
Week 2	Interview engineers, managers, and support stakeholders
Week 3	Synthesize findings and prioritize unmet outcomes
Week 4	Develop a low-fidelity prototype
Week 5	Conduct task-based usability testing
Week 6	Evaluate evidence and recommend whether to iterate, pilot, or stop
Potential success measures include:

Time required to locate an approved document.

First-time task completion rate.

Number and age of unresolved onboarding blockers.

User confidence in information accuracy.

Mentor time spent answering recurring questions.

Rate of unsupported or unsafe responses in test scenarios.

Targets would be defined after baseline measurement and stakeholder alignment.

Project status and transparency
MissionReady is an independent concept, not a commissioned client project or deployed product.

No interviews, customer research, prototype testing, or production implementation are claimed as completed. The problem statements, job map, ODI scores, solution capabilities, metrics, and timeline are proposed planning artifacts.

The next stage would be to validate the problem with real engineers, develop a low-fidelity prototype, and test whether the proposed experience creates measurable value.

Closing perspective
The central idea behind MissionReady is that effective onboarding should be measured not only by whether people complete required tasks, but also by whether they can confidently navigate the organization and make progress in their roles.

The opportunity is to connect people, processes, and trusted knowledge in a way that supports both individual effectiveness and organizational scalability.
